Screen-Free Summer Holiday Activities for Kids (That Actually Work)

🗓️ 30 July 2026
⏱️ 5 min read
✏️ Ages 4–9

It's the summer holidays, the "I'm bored" countdown has already started, and you're after screen-free summer holiday activities for kids that don't cost a penny and don't need a car journey to sort out. Good news: some of the best free things to do in the summer holidays are already sitting in your kitchen drawer. All you need is paper and a few coloured pencils.

Below are our favourite screen-free, budget-free ways to fill an hour (or three) with kids aged 4–9 — plus a free printable drawing pack you can download right now, featuring animals from both British gardens and the African savanna.

Why Drawing Is the Ultimate Free Summer Activity

Drawing ticks every box a bored-parent-of-bored-kids could ask for. It's completely free, it needs zero setup, it works at the kitchen table or in the back of the car, and it keeps little hands busy for far longer than you'd expect. Unlike most "free" activities, it doesn't quietly turn into a screen five minutes later either.

It's also quietly brilliant for kids: drawing builds concentration, fine motor skills, and — because every child can have a go regardless of ability — a real sense of "I made that!" confidence. That's the whole idea behind Chunky Badger's 6 Simple Steps method: break any animal down into easy shapes so children can draw independently, no reading required.

7 Screen-Free Things to Do With Kids This Summer

1. Learn to Draw an Animal in 6 Easy Steps

Start with our free pack above and pick an animal — a ladybird, a squirrel, a lion, or an elephant. Each one breaks down into 6 simple steps, so even first-time artists can follow along without help.

2. Turn the Garden Into a Sketching Safari

Head outside (or just look out the window) and see what you can spot — a robin, a bee, a spider in the shed. Sketch what you see, then look it up afterwards to see how close you got.

3. Run a Living Room "Animal Art Gallery"

Draw a few animals, colour them in, and Blu-Tack them up along the hallway or living room wall. Give your little artist a proper "gallery opening" — it costs nothing and the pride on their face is worth more than any subscription box.

4. Make Up a Story to Go With the Drawing

Once the animal is drawn, ask "what's their name, and what are they doing today?" A five-minute drawing can turn into a twenty-minute story session, and it's brilliant for language development too.

5. Have a Family Draw-Off

Everyone draws the same animal — mum, dad, siblings, grandparents on a video call — then compare results. It's silly, it's free, and it usually ends in a lot of laughing at Dad's wonky giraffe.

6. Build a Rainy-Afternoon Drawing Den

Cushions, a torch, a clipboard, and the free drawing pack. Ten minutes of setup buys you a solid hour of quiet, screen-free focus — perfect for the inevitable rainy day in the school holidays.

7. Post It to a Grandparent

A finished drawing in an envelope, sent to a grandparent or relative, costs a stamp and makes someone's day. It's a lovely screen-free way to fill a morning from start to finish — drawing, colouring, writing a little note, and a trip to the postbox.

What You'll Need (Spoiler: Not Much)

  • Plain paper (the free pack above is ready to print)
  • A pencil and eraser ✏️
  • Coloured pencils, crayons, or felt tips
  • A flat surface — kitchen table, bedroom floor, or garden picnic blanket

That's genuinely it. No subscriptions, no apps, no batteries.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are good screen-free activities for kids in the summer holidays?

Drawing, sketching outdoors, storytelling, building dens, and craft with things you already own are some of the best screen-free summer holiday activities for kids. They need little to no setup, cost nothing, and can run for as long (or as short) as your day allows.

What age are the drawing sheets suitable for?

Our step-by-step method is designed for children aged 4–9. Younger children may enjoy colouring in the finished animal while an older sibling or parent draws the steps, and older children can usually follow the 6 steps independently.

Do I need any special art supplies?

No — just plain paper, a pencil, an eraser, and some colouring pencils, crayons, or felt tips. Nothing needs to be bought specially.
