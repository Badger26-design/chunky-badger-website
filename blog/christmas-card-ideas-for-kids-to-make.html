Christmas Card Ideas
for Kids to Make
(Screen-Free)

🗓️ 16 November 2026
⏱️ 3 min read
✏️ Ages 4–9

Every year it's the same: a multipack of shop-bought cards appears in the hallway, everyone signs their name, and by January they're all in the recycling. Except one. There's always one wonky, over-glittered, slightly smudged card that Granny keeps on the mantelpiece until Easter — and it's always the one a child made themselves.

The trouble is that most Christmas card ideas for kids to make seem to involve glitter glue, forty minutes of setup and a kitchen table you can't use for two days. These ones don't. All six designs below are drawing-led, need almost nothing beyond a pencil and some colours, and are simple enough that a four-year-old can get going without an adult hovering. 🎨

Why a Drawn Card Beats a Shop-Bought One

Beyond the obvious (free, and far nicer), drawing a card is one of those rare activities that keeps a child genuinely busy without a screen in sight. There's a real reason to do it — someone is actually going to open it — which turns "do some drawing" into a proper little job with a finished result.

It's also brilliantly low-stakes practice. Nobody expects a Christmas card to look professional, so children relax, have a go, and end up far braver with their pencils than they would be on a blank page. That's the bit that sticks around long after December.

What You'll Need

  • Plain card, or A4 paper folded in half (perfectly fine)
  • A pencil, a rubber and a sharpener
  • Coloured pencils or felt tips
  • Optional: a white gel pen for snow, and a glue stick

Six Easy Christmas Card Designs Kids Can Draw

1

A Robin on a Snowy Branch 🐦

The classic, and the easiest — a robin is really just two round shapes with an orange chest. Add one bare branch and a few dots of snow and the card is done.

2

A Hedgehog in a Stripy Scarf 🦔

Draw a simple dome shape, add a pointy face, then flick in the spikes. Wrap a scarf round the neck in whatever colours your child fancies. Ridiculously cute, almost impossible to get wrong.

3

A Fox in the Snow 🦊

A pointy-eared head, a triangle nose and a big bushy tail curled round. Leave the bottom of the card white for snow and let the orange really pop.

4

A Reindeer From Two Circles 🦌

One circle for the head, a smaller one for the muzzle, two long ears and a pair of branchy antlers. One red dot for the nose and everyone knows exactly who it is.

5

A Stack of Baubles 🎄

Three or four circles down the middle of the card, each with a little square hook on top. Then fill each one with a different pattern — stripes, spots, zigzags. Lovely for younger children who want colouring more than drawing.

6

A Snowy Winter Scene ❄️

One triangle tree, one small house with a square window, a wobbly line of snow along the bottom. Add a robin in the corner if there's room. This is the one to use when a child wants to make eight cards quickly.

If your child wants their animals to look a bit more like the real thing, our step-by-step tutorials for the robin, hedgehog and fox break each one into six simple steps they can follow on their own.

Three Tips to Keep It Stress-Free

  • Fold the card first, then draw. Nothing stings like a finished drawing that turns out to be upside down on the back.
  • Do one practice sheet. Let them draw the design once on scrap paper. Confidence goes up, and so does the quality of the real one.
  • Let wonky be wonky. Resist the urge to "just fix" the reindeer's antlers. The wonky one is the one that gets kept. ✅

Make It a Little Tradition

Set up the kit on the table one cosy evening in late November with something on in the background, and let everyone make two or three cards. It takes half an hour, costs nothing, and you'll have the nicest cards on anyone's mantelpiece. Our favourite version: keep one card back each year with the date on it, and you'll have a record of your child's drawing growing up alongside them. 🎉
