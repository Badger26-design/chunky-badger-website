How to Draw a Robin
for Kids

🗓️ 08 November 2026
⏱️ 3 min read
✏️ Ages 4–9

There's no bird more British than the robin. That flash of orange-red on a frosty garden fence, the cheeky hop across the lawn, the way they seem to follow you about while you're digging — robins are everywhere this time of year, which makes them the perfect little model for a winter art session. Better still, learning how to draw a robin for kids is genuinely easy, because a robin is really just two circles wearing a lovely red waistcoat.

This tutorial uses the Chunky Badger 6 Simple Steps method: we start with basic shapes, add the big features, then finish with the fun details. Each step builds gently on the one before, so children can follow along independently — no reading required, and no artistic experience needed from grown-ups either. 🎨

What You'll Need

  • ✏️ A pencil (an HB is perfect)
  • 🧹 An eraser — mistakes are part of drawing!
  • ✏️ A sharpener
  • 🎨 Coloured pencils or felt-tip pens for the finish
  • 📄 A sheet of plain paper

That's it. No fancy supplies, no setting up — just find a cosy spot by the window and get started. Wondering about age ranges? Our FAQs have you covered.

Let's Draw a Robin! ✏️

1

Step 1: Draw the Head

Start near the top of your paper with a circle, roughly the size of a £2 coin. This is your robin's head. Robins have lovely round heads, so don't worry about making it neat — a slightly wobbly circle actually looks more like a real bird.

Keep your pencil strokes light — it's easy to erase!
2

Step 2: Add the Body

Underneath the head, draw a bigger oval that overlaps it slightly, tilting down and to the right. Think of an egg lying on its side. Robins are plump, puffed-up little birds in winter, so make this oval nice and round — the rounder the better.

If your robin looks like a snowman, you're doing it right!
3

Step 3: Add the Beak and Tail

Now for the two shapes that turn your circles into a bird. On the left side of the head, draw a small triangle pointing outwards — that's the beak. Then on the right side of the body, add a wider, flatter triangle for the tail, angled slightly upwards. Robins hold their tails perked up when they're curious, so a cheeky upward tilt gives your bird lots of personality.

4

Step 4: Add the Wing and Legs

Draw a curved teardrop shape on the side of the body for the wing — it should sit in the middle, like a folded jacket sleeve. Then add two thin legs at the bottom of the body, with three little toes on each foot.

Robin legs are skinny, like twigs. One quick line for each is plenty!
5

Step 5: Add the Details

This is where your robin really comes alive. Add a round eye near the front of the head and fill it in, leaving a tiny white dot for the sparkle. Draw a soft, curved line across the chest to mark where the red breast ends and the pale tummy begins. Then add a few short feather lines on the wing and tail. If you'd like your robin to be standing on something, draw a simple branch or a snowy fence post under its feet.

6

Step 6: Colour Your Robin In! 🎉

Time for the best bit. Follow the colouring guide below, press lightly for the pale areas and build up the colour slowly. Layering is the secret to lovely-looking feathers.

Your Robin Colouring Guide

Face & chest: warm orange-red (robins aren't post-box red — they're more like a satsuma)
Back, wing & tail: soft brown with a hint of olive green
Tummy: cream or very pale grey — leave it almost white
Beak & legs: dark grey-brown
Eye: black, with that little white sparkle left uncoloured

Well Done, Little Artist! 🎉

You've just drawn a robin — one of Britain's best-loved birds. Try drawing a few more in different poses: one hopping, one with its feathers puffed right up against the cold, one perched on a snowy branch. Pop one in a homemade Christmas card and you've got a present sorted too.

And here's a lovely follow-on activity: hang your finished drawing by the window, then see how many real robins you can spot in the garden this week. Drawing an animal is one of the best ways to really look at it properly.
